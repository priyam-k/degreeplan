# UMD Mathematics B.S.: Degree Requirements Research

Researched 2026-10-21. Primary sources were fetched as raw HTML and parsed directly. They were not summarized from memory.

- **Catalog years covered:** 2025-2026 (Fall 2025 entrants) and 2026-2027 (current live catalog). The **upper-level MATH/AMSC/STAT rules, the intro sequence, the programming requirement and the grade rules are identical in both years.** The only differences in the major page are a few **supporting-sequence course lists** (biology, astronomy, geology) and some course titles. See section 7.
- **Source keys** (full URLs in section 9): **[CAT26]** is the 2026-27 catalog major page. **[CAT25]** is the 2025-26 catalog major page (Wayback snapshot 2026-02-02, footer "© 2025-2026"). **[DEPT]** is the Math department's "Course Requirements" page. **[CRS26]**/**[CRS25]** are the catalog course descriptions. The other keys are defined in section 9.
- **Authority note:** [DEPT] says *"The course requirements listed below are for informational purposes only. Math majors should refer to their degree audits (available via Testudo)."* The catalog is the binding document. Where [DEPT] differs from the catalog, the difference is flagged.

#### 2.1 Lower level

#### 2.2 Upper level: "Select eight 400-level or higher [MATH/AMSC/STAT courses]; must include:"

Let **U** be the set of the student's distinct passed courses (C- or better) that are MATH/AMSC/STAT, numbered ≥ 400, and not on the G5 blacklist. Rules:

| Slot | Requirement | Allowed courses (exhaustive) |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | MATH410 Advanced Calculus I | **MATH410** |
| A2 | STAT410 Intro to Probability Theory | **STAT410** (cross-listed with SURV410; only one of the two earns credit) |
| A3 | One additional STAT course, **not STAT400, STAT410 or STAT464** | Any STAT ≥ 400 except 400/410/464. Current 400-level catalog list: **STAT401, STAT420, STAT422, STAT426, STAT430, STAT440, STAT470, STAT498** (selected topics). STAT6xx+ also qualifies under G4. |
| A4 | Linear algebra / optimization: one of | **MATH401, MATH405, MATH423** (note: *not* MATH403, unlike the Traditional track) |
| A5 | Numerical: one of | **AMSC460, AMSC466** (cross-listed as **CMSC460, CMSC466**). Credit is granted for only one of AMSC460/AMSC466/CMSC460/CMSC466 [CRS26]. |
| A6 | Applied elective: one of | **MATH416, MATH420, MATH424, MATH431, MATH452, MATH456, MATH462, MATH463, MATH464, MATH475**. Cross-listed equivalents: AMSC420≡MATH420, AMSC452≡MATH452, CMSC456/ENEE456≡MATH456, CMSC475≡MATH475. |
| A7 | **Depth requirement**: a one-year sequence, i.e. **both** courses of one pair | (i) **MATH410 + MATH411**; (ii) **MATH410 + MATH463**; (iii) **MATH416 + MATH464**; (iv) **MATH462 + MATH463**; (v) **STAT410 + STAT420** |
| A8 | Electives | Any course in U. The catalog line reads "Select electives: 400-level MATH/AMSC/STAT course" (footnote 3: "400 level courses. May not include: MATH461, MATH478, MATH480-MATH484, STAT464"). |
| COUNT | `|U_used| ≥ 8` | The 8 must be **distinct courses** (credit-only-granted-for equivalents count once). |
| RES | ≥ 4 of the 8 taken at UMD | G3 |

**Encoding notes (applied track).**
- Slots A1-A6 are each filled by a **distinct** course. Their allowed sets are pairwise disjoint, so no single course can fill two of A1-A6.
- **A7 may reuse courses already used in A1-A6.** For example, MATH410 is both A1 and half of pair (i); STAT410 is A2 and half of (v); MATH463/462/416/464 may be A6 and half of a pair; STAT420 may be A3 and half of (v). The catalog prices A7 at **3 credits**, i.e. usually one new course.
- Whatever is left over up to 8 distinct courses is elective (A8).
- Minimum structure: 6 slot courses (A1-A6), plus 0-1 new course for depth, plus electives to reach 8.
- Example minimal plan: MATH410, STAT410, STAT420 (A3 and depth v), MATH401, AMSC460, MATH420, plus 2 electives = 8.
- Pseudo-constraint:
  ```
  pick distinct a1..a6 from U per slot sets A1..A6
  exists pair P in DEPTH_PAIRS with P ⊆ U
  |U_selected| >= 8, where U_selected ⊇ {a1..a6} ∪ P
  count(c in U_selected where c.taken_at_UMD) >= 4
  ```
- **MATH462 double use:** if MATH462 fills L6 (instead of MATH246), [DEPT] says *"it may also be used as one of the upper level math requirements in (3)(f)"*, i.e. as A6 and as one of the 8. MATH462 requires MATH246 (or MATH341/MATH243/ENEE290) as a prerequisite, so in practice this only applies to someone who took 246 without it counting, or took 243/ENEE290.
- **Outside-department substitution** (two 400-level outside courses with strong math content, MATH141 prerequisite, replacing one elective, with Undergraduate Office approval): the catalog **attaches this only to the Traditional track's footnote 3**. [DEPT] also puts it only under Traditional. **Do not assume it applies to Applied** (see Uncertainties).

## (b) Tricky rules

- **T1. Depth-pair overlap.** In the Applied track, the depth pair may overlap A1/A2/A3/A6. Count 8 *distinct* courses overall; a course in two slots counts once toward the 8.
- **T2. A3 is "STAT other than 400/410/464".** STAT400 does **not** satisfy A3, but STAT400 does still count as a generic 400-level elective (it is not blacklisted). For the Traditional track, STAT400 *does* satisfy the STAT slot (only STAT464 is excluded there).
- **T3. MATH246 replacement in the Applied track.**
  - The catalog (both years) offers MATH246 / MATH436 / MATH462.
  - [DEPT]'s Applied section mentions only MATH462 (and 340-341).
  - The catalog governs, but flag MATH436 for the Applied track as "verify on audit".
  - If MATH462 fills L6, [DEPT] allows it to also fill A6 and count among the 8.
- **T4. MATH461.** MATH461 can satisfy the MATH240 requirement (L3) per [DEPT], but it is **blacklisted** as an upper-level course. Only one of 240/341/461 earns credit.
- **T5. AMSC/CMSC 460/466 "credit only granted for" all four.** A student can earn credit for only **one** of AMSC460, AMSC466, CMSC460, CMSC466, so they cannot take both 460 and 466 for credit.
  - For the Math applied track, A5 is satisfied by whichever code the student registered under; CMSC460/466 are the same cross-listed course.
  - For CS, the same course fills **CS Area 5 (Numerical Analysis)** upper-level CMSC ([CS-CAT]: "CMSC460 or CMSC466; credit will only be given for CMSC460 or CMSC466").
  - Whether the Math audit accepts the CMSC-coded registration is not stated; see Uncertainties.
- **T6. Programming requirement vs. supporting sequence (CS double majors).**
  - CMSC131 or CMSC132 satisfies L7, and CMSC131+132+216 is supporting Sequence 4.
  - Neither source says whether a course may satisfy both L7 and Sequence 4 at once.
  - The Statistics/Traditional sample plan shows "CMSC106 or 131" in year 1 as programming, plus a separate supporting sequence.
  - **Conservative encoding:** allow the overlap only with a warning. A strictly disjoint reading would need CMSC106 (or another L7 course) in addition to 131/132/216. Ask the advisor.
- **T7. MATH310** is required but is **not** an upper-level course for the major. It can be exempted (L5 conditions). It is also the gate for MATH410/405 (and in 2026-27, indirectly MATH403).
- **T8. MATH340/341 credit math.** The honors pair is 8 credits replacing 11 credits (240+241+246), so the major's credit totals drop. MATH341 also satisfies the Secondary-Ed "one of" list directly.
- **T9. Graduate courses** (6xx+) in MATH/AMSC/STAT count toward the eight upper-level courses ("400-level or higher").
- **T10. Cross-listed duplicates.** MATH420/AMSC420 and MATH452/AMSC452 are one course each. Count them once and accept either code in A6 (the A6 list names only the MATH codes).
- **T11. Grade threshold applies to supporting courses too.** CMNS: "A 'C-' or higher is required in all major courses (unless specified otherwise)", and the major GPA ≥ 2.0 is over "all courses used to satisfy major degree requirements". The programming and supporting-sequence courses are listed as major requirements, so they almost certainly fall under C- and the GPA rule.
- **T12. Benchmarks** ([CMNS-BENCH]; Applied/Traditional/Statistics tracks; C- or better; counted in semesters *in the major*). Failing benchmarks can lead to dismissal from the major.
  - By the 2nd semester: MATH140, MATH141.
  - By the 4th semester: MATH240, MATH241, MATH246, MATH310, and the computer science (programming) requirement.
  - By the 6th semester: MATH410 and two 400-level AMSC/MATH/STAT courses.
- **T13. Declaring the double major** ([CMNS-DECL], [DEPT-ADV]):
  - The double major must be officially added **one year before graduation**.
  - First-semester students must wait until first-semester grades post before declaring a double major with Math.
  - The Math office does not accept new majors from mid-October to early December, from mid-March to early May, or during the start-of-term window.
  - Every Math major must complete mandatory advising each semester.

### Double counting / double major (CS + Math + Robotics minor)

- **D1. University: no overlap cap between two majors.** [CAT-DEG] "Second Majors" requires completing "all of the requirements specified for both the primary and secondary major". It sets **no limit and no unique-credit minimum** for a double major under one degree. A course may count toward both majors when it meets each major's rule.
  - The **"at least 18 credits applied to one degree must not be applied to the other" rule and the 150-credit minimum apply only to double *degrees*** (two bachelor's degrees) [CAT-DEG]. CS and Math are both B.S. programs in CMNS, so a CS+Math student is normally a double *major* (one B.S.). Confirm the student's record isn't a dual degree.
  - No CMNS or Math-department double-major overlap limit was found ([CMNS-POL], [CMNS-DECL], [DEPT], [FAQ] checked).
- **D2. Minor-major overlap cap (relevant to the Robotics minor):** "No more than six credits (or two courses) may be applied to satisfy both the requirements of a minor and a major program", and "No course may be used to satisfy the requirements of more than one minor" ([CAT-DEG], stated as true "with rare exceptions"). Whether the 2-course cap is per major or total across both majors is not specified.
- **D3. CS-side rules affecting Math overlap** ([CS-CAT], [CS-ULC]):
  - CS requires **STAT4xx** and one **MATH/AMSC/STAT xxx** course ("must have prerequisite of MATH141 or higher; cannot be cross-listed with CMSC"). So AMSC460/466 cannot be the CS math course, while STAT410, MATH240/241/246, etc. can. These may also count for Math (no prohibition found). Example: STAT410 = CS STAT4xx = Math A2.
  - CS upper-level areas include CMSC456 (≡MATH456, Area 4 Theory) and CMSC460/466 (≡AMSC460/466, Area 5). These cross-listed courses can satisfy a CS area slot and a Math slot (A5; A6 for 456).
  - **CS Upper Level Concentration (ULC)** means 12 credits at the 300-400 level in one discipline outside CMSC; MATH and STAT may be mixed. "Students pursuing a minor or a double major/dual degree may use those courses towards their ULC". So **Math-major MATH/STAT courses can double as the CS ULC**, except:
    - courses in or cross-listed with CMSC, or credit-only-granted-for with CMSC: **AMSC460, AMSC466, MATH456, MATH475, STAT426**;
    - also explicitly ineligible: **MATH440, MATH461, STAT464**;
    - the STAT4xx used for the CS math requirement;
    - any course already used for the CS major itself (e.g., the CS "MATH/AMSC/STAT xxx" course).
  - ULC needs a cumulative GPA ≥ 1.7 (D allowed); at most 6 transfer credits; at most 3 credits of independent study.
  - **Robotics minor courses** (ENAE450, ENEE467, CMSC477, etc.) are ineligible for the ULC.
  - "ULC does not count toward the 6 credits/2 courses that can overlap between a major and minor."
